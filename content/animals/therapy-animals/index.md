## What Is a Therapy Animal

- A trained animal that provides comfort and therapeutic benefits to multiple people in various settings such as hospitals, schools, and nursing homes
- Unlike service animals they are not individually trained to perform tasks for one person with a disability
- Trained to be friendly- therapy animals are selected for their good temperament and are trained to be people friendly and calm
- Visit various locations-they visit hospitals, nursing homes, schools, libraries, and other public places to bring comfort and joy
- Not Federally protected-unlike service animals, therapy animals and their handlers do not have a government ID and are not allowed in all public places

## How Therapy Animals Help

- Reduce stress and anxiety - therapy animals have a calming effect that can lower stress and anxiety levels in people
- Improve emotional well-being - they can increase self-esteem, lower loneliness, and improve a person's overall mood
- Encourage socialization - Therapy animals help people make friends and talk to each other.
- Assist in Physical Therapy - Therapy animals help people move better and get stronger
- Support kids in school - Therapy dogs help kids feel better at school, learn more, and make friends.

## How to Get a Therapy Animal

1. Prepare your pet and yourself-
   - Train and socialize - both you and your animal need to be a good fit for therapy work
   - Meet age requirements - the animal must be usually at least one year old
   - Complete foundational training - Earn the AKC Canine Good Citizen certification, which has a test at the end
   - Pass health requirements - makes sure your therapy dog is healthy and has gotten their shots from the vet.
2. Get certified
   - Research organizations - find a therapy animal group near you and see if it is the right fit for you.
   - Pass the test - the dog and its owner need to show they can work well together and be calm in different environments
   - Register - Sign up by sending in your dog's health papers and paying any fees

## The Cost and Training of a Therapy Animal

- Basic Obedience Classes are $250 to $500 for 6-8 weeks in a group class. Special training where your pet stays with a trainer can cost $1,500 or more.
- Therapy Animal Prep Course are $150 to $200 for special classes to get your pet ready.
- Handler Education Courses are $80 for learning how to work with your pet, sometimes from big pet groups.
- Evaluations & Certifications are $30 to $100 to test you and your pet and to sign up

## Can Insurance Cover the Cost?

- Health Insurance does not cover any therapy animal expenses, regardless of whether it is a pet or a registered therapy animal.
- Pet Insurance does not cover basic obedience or certification training. However, some policies like those from Embrace Pet Insurance or Fetch Pet Insurance do cover veterinary-prescribed behavioral therapy for underlying issues like anxiety or aggression.

## Places to Obtain a Therapy Animal

There is no place to buy an already trained therapy animal. You have to train your pet yourself to be a therapy animal.

## Therapy Animal Rules by Airlines

- American Airlines
  - Therapy animals and ESAs must pay a pet fee of $150 and keep the animal in a carrier
- Delta Airlines
  - Does not recognize ESAs or therapy animals as service animals. They may travel as pets for a fee (approx. $95 for domestic) if they fit in a ventilated carrier under the seat.
- Southwest Airlines
  - Only recognizes trained service dogs. All other support animals are treated as pets
- United Airlines
  - Treats therapy animals as pets. In-cabin travel is limited to cats and dogs that fit in a carrier for a $150 fee per way
- JetBlue
  - Treats therapy animals as pets. They are required to travel in FAA approved carriers under the seat with a fee of $125 each way
- Frontier Airlines
  - Treats therapy animals as pets. They are required to travel in a carrier under the seat with a fee of $99 each way

## Can Therapy Animals Go Into Restaurants and Stores?

No, therapy animals are not allowed everywhere like service animals. Stores and restaurants can say no to therapy animals. The law does not protect them the same way, so it is up to the owner if they can come inside.

## Where Are They Used?

- Hospitals
- Schools
- Rehabilitation centers (Places that help people feel better after being sick or hurt)
- Nursing Homes (Homes where older people get care and help)
- Courtrooms and Courthouses (Places where people talk about important things or meet to solve problems)

## What to Do When You See a Therapy Animal in Public

- The most important rule is to always ask the handler for permission before interacting. They are still "on the job" when in public and should not be distracted without consent.
- Never pet, talk to, or offer treats to the animal without first asking the handler.
- Make eye contact with and speak directly to the handler rather than the animal.
- If a handler declines your request for interaction, they may be on their way to a scheduled session, or the animal may need a break.
- Avoid crowding the animal or blocking its path.
- If you have your own pet with you, maintain a respectful distance to avoid distracting the therapy animal
