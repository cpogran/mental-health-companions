## What Is a Service Animal?

- A service animal is a special helper animal that learns to do specific tasks for a person who needs help. These animals can go places that other animals cannot, like stores and restaurants.
- They are considered working animals, not pets, and cannot be denied access to places the public is allowed
- Staff at stores can only ask two questions to confirm:
  1. If the animal is required due to a disability
  2. What task it performs
- They cannot ask about the person's disability or demand documentation

## How to Get a Service Animal

1. See if you qualify- you must have a physical, sensory, psychiatric, or other mental disability that substantially limits a major life activity.
2. Pick Your Learning and Joining Path- you can either go to an organization that trains service dogs and apply for one and get matched with an animal or you can train your own animal for your disability

## Diagnoses That Would Qualify Someone for a Service Animal

1. PTSD (Post Traumatic Stress Disorder)
2. Anxiety and Panic Disorders
3. Autism Spectrum Disorder
4. Major Depression
5. Schizophrenia
6. OCD
7. Epilepsy and Seizure Disorders
8. Diabetes
9. Cardiac conditions
10. Narcolepsy
11. Multiple Sclerosis
12. Mobility impairments
13. Visual impairments
14. Hearing impairments

## Common Tasks Service Animals Are Trained to Do

- Guiding people who are blind or visually impaired
- Alerting a person who is deaf or hard of hearing to sounds
- Assisting with balance and stability or pulling a wheelchair
- Providing physical support during a seizure
- Reminding a person to take medication
- Interfering with impulsive or destructive behaviors

## Cost of Getting and Training a Service Animal

Getting and training a service animal typically costs between $15,000 and $50,000, heavily depending on whether you purchase a fully trained dog from an organization or owner-train one with professional guidance.

## Can Insurance Cover the Cost?

- Providers like Medicare, Medicaid, and private commercial health plans classify service dogs as "personal expenses" and do not cover acquisition or training costs
- Veterans Affairs allows qualified veterans with a prescription for a certified guide or service dog to have the animal's veterinary care, treatments, and associated medications covered

## Fun Facts!

- The task that the service animal is trained for must be related to the individual's disability
- Emotional support, comfort, or companionship alone does not qualify a dog as a service animal according to the law
- They also have no legal rights on personal property
- Allergies and fear of dogs are not valid reasons to deny access

## Key Rights and Rules

- Access
  - Service animals are allowed anywhere the public can go including restaurants, stores, schools, and government buildings
- No identification required
  - They do not need to be registered, licensed, or wear vests or ID cards, though some handlers may carry identification
- No documentation demands
  - Staff cannot ask for proof of training, medical records, or a demonstration of the dog's abilities

## Can Service Animals Go Into Restaurants?

Yes, service animals are legally allowed to go into stores. Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, privately-owned businesses that serve the public must allow service animals to accompany people with disabilities anywhere customers are generally allowed.

## When Can a Service Animal Be Excluded

- If the animal is not housebroken
- If the animal is out of control (growling, biting, or excessive barking)
- If the animal being there would change or interfere with what is supposed to happen.

## What to Do When You See a Service Animal in Public

- Do not pet or distract a working service animal without permission from the handler
- Do not pet, talk to, or otherwise distract the dog, as it is on duty, which is crucial for its handler's safety and independence.
- Avoid eye contact, petting, or talking to the animal.
- Ensure your own pets do not approach or distract the service dog.
- Do not feed the dog, as it can disrupt its training and diet.
- If a service dog approaches you without its handler, it may be looking for help. Follow the dog, as it might be trying to lead you to its owner.
